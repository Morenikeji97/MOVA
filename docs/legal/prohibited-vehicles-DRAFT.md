# ShipMova Prohibited Vehicles Policy — DRAFT for attorney review

*Draft v0.1 · prepared 2026-10-09 · not reviewed by a licensed attorney · not published on shipmova.com*

> **For the attorney.** This policy lists vehicles that may not be listed on ShipMova, and vehicles that may be listed only with extra disclosure. It sits alongside the Terms & Conditions (§8 Prohibited Conduct) and the Seller Terms draft. The last column of each table says what ShipMova's software enforces today, so you can see where the policy relies on Seller honesty and staff review. Text in *[brackets]* is a decision for ShipMova; open questions are in Section 5.

---

## 1. Never allowed

These vehicles may not be listed, reserved or shipped through ShipMova. ShipMova removes any listing it finds, may close the Seller's account, and may report it to law enforcement or the National Insurance Crime Bureau (NICB).

| Vehicle | Why | Enforced today |
|---|---|---|
| Stolen, or reported stolen | Crime; U.S. export of a stolen vehicle is illegal (CBP checks the VIN at export) | Staff run NICB VINCheck at review |
| VIN missing, altered, defaced, duplicated, or not matching the title | Can't be verified or lawfully exported | VIN format check; duplicate-VIN rule (0044); staff review; the inspector reads the VIN on the car |
| No title, or the Seller doesn't control the title (lost, held by a lender with no payoff arranged, or under a title loan) | The vehicle can't be transferred or exported without the original title | Title photo required; staff compare it with the verified ID |
| **Junk, non-repairable, "parts only"**, or with a certificate of destruction | Can't be retitled or registered; banned as scrap in destination countries | Not yet: the title status list has no "junk" option *[add one, and refuse it automatically]* |
| Subject to an undischarged lien with no payoff arranged before handover | The lienholder owns an interest; export is refused without a lien release | Seller declaration only *[add a lien question to the listing form]* |
| Leased, rented or financed vehicle the Seller has no right to sell | Not the Seller's to sell | Authorization document required for non-owners; staff review |
| Vehicle the Seller has been told is the subject of a crime, insurance fraud or an investigation | Crime | Staff review |
| Any vehicle whose export from the U.S., or sale to the Buyer, would break U.S. sanctions or export law (including a Buyer in a comprehensively sanctioned country) | Law (OFAC, EAR) | Buyers verified by ID in Nigeria, Ghana, Togo or Benin only |
| Vehicles with a removed or tampered emissions control or airbag system, or a rolled-back odometer | Federal law; buyer safety | Inspector compares the odometer and the listing; staff review |
| **Right-hand-drive vehicles** *[decision]* | Nigeria and Ghana restrict right-hand-drive imports | Not enforced *[add a question, or accept left-hand drive only]* |
| Kit cars, and vehicles assembled from parts of different vehicles | Ghana bars "vehicles assembled from parts" (GSA, from 1 Oct 2026); title and VIN integrity | Staff review |
| Motorcycles, boats, trailers, RVs, buses, and heavy trucks over the size classes ShipMova supports | Outside what ShipMova and its shippers handle | The listing form only offers sedan and SUV/truck size classes |

## 2. Allowed only with full disclosure

These may be listed only if the listing states it plainly. Buyers see it before reserving.

| Vehicle | What the Seller must disclose | Enforced today |
|---|---|---|
| **Salvage, rebuilt, flood**, fire, hail or lemon/buyback title brand | The brand exactly as on the title, plus the damage history | Title status field; the listing shows it; Ghana import rules mark flood and salvage as "can't be imported to Ghana" |
| Prior accident or structural damage | Severity and repairs | Accident history field |
| Open safety recalls not yet repaired | Which recalls, and whether repaired | ShipMova shows NHTSA recalls for the model automatically; repair status relies on the Seller |
| A "do not drive" recall (NHTSA "park it") not yet repaired | Must be disclosed, and the car must not be driven to the port | Shown highlighted on the listing *[decision: prohibit until repaired?]* |
| Mileage not actual (exempt, not actual, exceeds mechanical limits) | The odometer status as on the title | *[add to the listing form]* |
| Former rental, fleet, police or taxi use | The history | Seller declaration |
| Too old for a destination country's import rules | Nothing extra: ShipMova shows each country's rule on the listing | Automatic per-country import checks (Nigeria 12 years, Ghana 15 years; Togo and Benin to be confirmed) |

*[Decision for ShipMova: should salvage, rebuilt and flood titles be allowed at all? Arguments for: many export buyers buy them knowingly, and they're legal to sell. Against: Ghana refuses salvage and flood, and they generate the most misrepresentation disputes. Option: allow them only for buyers in countries that accept them, shown as "can't be imported to Ghana".]*

## 3. What happens if a prohibited vehicle is listed

1. ShipMova rejects or removes the listing and tells the Seller why.
2. If a Buyer has already reserved or paid ShipMova's fee, ShipMova refunds that fee under the Buyer Protection & Refund Policy and tells Escrow.com to cancel any open escrow, if one exists.
3. ShipMova may suspend or close the Seller's account (Seller Terms §11).
4. Suspected theft, fraud or VIN tampering may be reported to law enforcement and NICB.

## 4. Destination-country rules

ShipMova shows each listing's import status for Nigeria, Ghana, Togo and Benin with its source and the date it was last checked. These are information for Buyers, not a ShipMova guarantee: the Buyer and their clearing agent are responsible for importing (Terms & Conditions §5). A vehicle that can't be imported to one country may still be listed for buyers in others.

## 5. Questions for the attorney

1. Does ShipMova have any duty to check for, or refuse, vehicles with open recalls (including "do not drive" recalls) before export?
2. Should ShipMova's policy refuse salvage, rebuilt and flood titles outright, given Ghana's ban and the dispute risk, or is disclosure enough?
3. The CBP export rule (original title, 72 hours' notice): what should ShipMova require from the Seller, versus the shipper, to keep this enforceable?
4. Liability if a prohibited vehicle gets through review (e.g. a stolen car with a clean NICB result): does the Terms' limitation of liability cover ShipMova?
5. Is reporting to NICB or police on suspicion lawful without the Seller's consent? Any defamation risk in removal reasons shown to the Seller?
6. Whether left-hand-drive-only should be a policy rule, given Nigeria and Ghana restrict right-hand-drive imports.
7. Whether this policy should be accepted separately by Sellers at listing time, or incorporated by reference in the Seller Terms.
